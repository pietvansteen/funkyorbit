How it works

How we pick the colours of the clubs and how the difficulty score is made. Type your own siteswap at the bottom to see the rules at work.

Orbits & colours

A pattern is made up of orbits: groups of throws that keep passing the same clubs around. A club thrown as a 9 lands where the next throw of its orbit starts, and so on, until it is back where it began. Each orbit gets its own club colour, so you can see what's going on.

An odd throw is a pass and an even throw is a self. The number of clubs in an orbit is the sum of its throws divided by the period: in 97586 (period 5), the orbit 9 + 6 holds (9 + 6) / 5 = 3 clubs.

All throws that are a multiple of the period form one orbit and share a fixed colour, however many of them there are: 9 and 6 in period 3, a and 5 in period 5, 7 in period 7. They always come back to the same juggler, so you recognise them at once in every pattern.

    Difficulty

    The score adds up how hard the throws are, plus extra points for tricky combinations. “In a row” means in the order one juggler throws them, so every second throw of the siteswap. A sync pattern is worked out for both jugglers and gets the score of the harder side.
